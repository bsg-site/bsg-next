questions:
  - q: "What are you working on now?"
    a: "My lab is interested in understanding how bacteria exchange genetic material and how viruses that infect bacteria can manipulate this process. We use cryo-electron microscopy to visualise the remarkable molecular machines involved, particularly those responsible for spreading antibiotic resistance and virulence. More recently, we are exploring how artificial intelligence can help us uncover biological mechanisms that might otherwise remain hidden."
  - q: "What did winning the BSG Early Career Prize mean for you, and what happened next?"
    a: "Receiving the BSG Early Career Prize in 2023 was a tremendous honour and an important moment in my career. It was particularly meaningful because it recognised not only my work but also the dedication and creativity of the students, postdocs and collaborators who made it possible. Since then, my group has expanded into exciting new areas of research, from discovering unexpected ways in which bacterial viruses exchange genetic material to exploring how AI can contribute to scientific discovery. Looking back, the prize came at a pivotal moment and gave me additional confidence to pursue ambitious scientific questions."
  - q: "What advice would you give someone starting out in structural biology?"
    a: "Don't fall in love with a technique; fall in love with a biological question. Structural biology is most powerful when combined with other approaches, so be curious, collaborate widely and don't be afraid to venture outside your comfort zone. Some of the most exciting discoveries emerge when experiments challenge what you thought you knew, so learn to embrace unexpected results rather than simply trying to confirm your initial hypothesis."
