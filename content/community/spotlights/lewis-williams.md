questions:
  - q: "What are you working on now?"
    a: "I have recently started a postdoc position at the University of Essex where I'm working on proteins involved in motor neuron disease (MND). I'm particularly interested in the interactions of these proteins with dipeptide repeats which are produced during MND and hope to produce structural and kinetic characterisations of these interactions. The long term goal is to use de novo protein design to produce binders to disrupt the protein-peptide interactions."
  - q: "What did winning the David Blow Poster Prize mean for you, and what happened next?"
    a: "I attended two BCA meetings during my PhD, on both occasions, I was fortunate enough to present a poster and to give a talk at the ESCG Early Career satellite meetings. These were fantastic opportunities to share my work with the community and led to many interesting conversations with people I may not have met otherwise. It's always nice to receive recognition and winning the David Blow poster prize was definitely an added highlight to what was an already hugely rewarding meeting. Since then, I have published some more papers based on work presented at the BCA, finished my PhD, and have begun a postdoc position."
  - q: "What advice would you give someone starting out in structural biology?"
    a: "While I'm still fairly early on in my career, I'd say continuing to try and learn as much as you can about whatever you're doing is key to building a foundation of knowledge from which you can pursue your ideas. Always be open to new experiences, techniques, and perspectives; these can all lead you to new places in your research. Finally, have fun and enjoy what you're doing."
